Current Status of Bhumij Tribe

Population

~1 Million (Est. Census Data)

Regions Found

West Bengal, Odisha, Jharkhand (Chota Nagpur Plateau / Singhbhum region), Assam, Bihar, and parts of Bangladesh

Status of the Tribe

Recognized Scheduled Tribe (ST) in India; Austroasiatic / Munda ethnic lineage with officially recognized language status in Jharkhand (Ol Onol Script)

Bhumij Heritage & Data Index

Goram-Gohram/ Singbonga Deity

Religion, Cosmology, Sun Deity, Nature Worship

Supreme creator deity in Bhumij spiritual cosmology, representing the Sun, purity, and life-giving energy. Sacred offerings are made to seek village protection and rain.

Source: Tribal Life in India, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting

Coverage: Chota Nagpur Plateau, Jharkhand, India

Creators: Bhumij Tribal Community

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Kili / Gotra System

Social Structure, Exogamous, Clans, Totemism, Kinship

The totemic social organization of the Bhumij tribe divided into exogamous clans (Kili/Gotras) named after flora and fauna like Sal tree (Tula), Turtle (Kachhap), and Serpent (Nag).

Source: The Tribes and Castes of Bengal, Risley H.H.

Coverage: East Singhbhum, Jharkhand, India

Creators: Ancestral Lineage Elders

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Bhumij Language(Latar/Tar Munda)

Linguistics, Austroasiatic, Munda, Languages, Oral Tradition

An Austroasiatic language belonging to the Munda sub-family spoken by the Bhumij people in Jharkhand, West Bengal, and Odisha. Primarily preserved through oral folktales.

Source: Linguistic Survey of India, Vol IV (Munda and Dravidian Languages)

Coverage: Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, India

Creators: Bhumij Speakers

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Ol Onol Script

Writing System, Script Revival, Indigenous Literacy

The writing script created specifically for writing the Bhumij language to preserve oral literature, folklore, and educational materials from extinction.

Source: Indigenous Literacy and Script Movements in Eastern India

Coverage: Seraikela Kharsawan, Jharkhand

Creators: Mahendra Nath Sardar

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Jaher Than

Sacred Grove, Forest Ecology, Biodiversity, Nature Temple

Sacred virgin forest groves maintained at the border of Bhumij villages. Cutting trees or harming animals in Jaher Than is strictly prohibited, preserving indigenous biodiversity.

Source: Sacred Groves and Ethno-Forestry practices among Munda and Bhumij Tribes

Coverage: Mayurbhanj & Singhbhum Forest Belt, India

Creators: Village Elders & Layas

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Laya (Traditional Village Priest)

Traditional Governance, Sacred Rituals, Spiritual Priest

The traditional village priest responsible for conducting sacred rituals at Jaher Than, leading seasonal festivals, and maintaining harmony between villagers and nature spirits.

Source: Customary Laws and Governance of Chota Nagpur Tribes

Coverage: Purulia & Singhbhum Region, India

Creators: Hereditary Village Priests

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Sarhul Festival

Spring Blossom, Flower Festival, Agriculture Cycle

Major spring festival celebrating the flowering of Sal trees. Villagers offer Sal blossoms to deities and ancestors at Jaher Than before starting agricultural activity.

Source: Festival Traditions of Tribal Jharkhand

Coverage: Chota Nagpur Region, Jharkhand

Creators: Community Folk Groups

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Karam Festival

Autumn Harvest, Karam Tree, Worship, Fertility Ritual

Autumn harvest festival centered on worshipping the branch of the Karam tree (Nauclea parvifolia). Youth dance overnight to celebrate agricultural growth, prosperity, and sisterhood.

Source: Ethnobotanical Sacred Trees of Eastern India

Coverage: Ranchi, Purulia and Singhbhum Districts

Creators: Youth & Village Elders

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Sohrai Festival

Wall Art, Post-Harvest Celebration

Post-harvest winter festival honoring farm animals. Homes are painted with natural mud pigments and cattle are bathed, anointed with oils, and decorated with garlands.

Source: Indigenous Folk Paintings and Cattle Festivals of Jharkhand

Coverage: East Singhbhum, Jharkhand & West Bengal

Creators: Bhumij Farmers

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Bandna Festival

Cattle Worship, Post Harvest Celebration

Bandna is a traditional harvest-season festival celebrated by agrarian communities—especially Santhal, Kurmi, Mahato, and other farming groups. It centers on the belief that cattle and agricultural tools are essential partners in human survival, and thus deserve respect and ritual care.

Source: Jharkhand Government

Coverage: East Singhbhum, Jharkhand & West Bengal

Creators: Bhumij People

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Dishua Hadi Bonga

Nature Worship

Hadi Bonga is associated with nature worship, ancestral reverence, and community celebration. It is closely tied to Sarhul, the spring festival that honors Jaher Era, the deity of forests and nature.

Source: Alamy

Coverage: East Singhbhum, Jharkhand & West Bengal

Creators: Bhumij People

Contributors: Hribhendu Ranjan Keshri, Ashish Kumar, Natansh Parth

Ganga Narayan Singh & Chuar Rebellion(1832)

Freedom Movement, Chuar Rebellion, Tribal Resistance

Historical 1832 armed uprising led by Bhumij leader Ganga Narayan Singh against oppressive British land revenues and colonial expansion in Dhalbhum and Manbhum.

Source: The Chuar Rebellion and Bhumij Revolt of 1832-33, J.C. Jha

Coverage: Manbhum, Dhalbhum, Jungle Mahals, India
